Where Linux comes from

It started as a hobby project in 1991 and turned into the most widely used operating system in the world (most people just don't realise it)

Some history

A Finnish student
started this as a project

Linus Torvalds wanted to mess around with his new PC, so he wrote a kernel (software that talks to the hardware). He posted about it on a Usenet group, other people got interested, and the rest is just history :)

By the mid-90s, Linux was running on servers. Then phones (Android is Linux btw). Then supercomputers. Desktops took longer, but many run Linux now

You can read the code

Anyone can look at how Linux works, find bugs, fix them, or just learn from it. (it is licensed under the GNU Public License)

People contribute (voluntarily)

Students, hobbyists, employees at big companies all contribute

It doesn't get abandoned

When companies stop selling or supporting products, they die, just like Windows 10. Linux keeps going and improving because people depend on it

It just works on old machines

Because the community doesn't need to sell you new hardware, there's no reason to make it run worse (or not at all) on older computers.
